Complete Shutter Down Paralyzes Rawalakot Poonch as Thousands Protest Against Punitive Measures, Power Cuts and Communication Blackouts

Rawalakot, Poonch, Azad Kashmir | 16 December 2025

A complete shutter down brought life to a standstill in Rawalakot on Monday as thousands of residents from across District Poonch poured into the streets to protest prolonged power outages, severe load shedding, low voltage electricity, and persistent internet and mobile network disruptions.

Markets remained closed and traffic was largely halted as protesters marched through the city and gathered near the grid station, demanding an immediate end to what they described as collective punishment of the population. The protest reflected growing public anger over months of worsening electricity shortages and communication blackouts that have made daily life increasingly unbearable.

Residents say Azad Kashmir has been subjected to extreme load shedding for several months, with the Poonch region facing between 15 and 20 hours of power cuts daily. Electricity supply remains unstable, voltage levels are dangerously low, and internet and mobile services are frequently disrupted or slowed to near unusable levels.

The impact has been widespread. Small businesses dependent on electricity and digital connectivity are struggling to survive. Hospitals, clinics, schools, and universities report serious disruptions. Printing services, legal offices, students, teachers, and research workers have all been affected. Economic activity across the region has been pushed to the brink.

Millions of Kashmiris living abroad are also feeling the impact. Families report spending hours trying to reach loved ones, often without success, due to prolonged power outages and failing communication networks.

Public frustration has been building for more than two and half years, during which residents across Azad Kashmir have repeatedly protested peacefully for access to basic services. These protests previously forced authorities to enter into agreements promising electricity at production cost and improvements in essential infrastructure.

Protesters accuse the authorities of responding to legitimate democratic demands with force, intimidation, and disinformation. Peaceful demonstrations in recent years have been met with violent crackdowns, resulting in multiple civilian deaths, including incidents in Muzaffarabad in May 2024 and further fatalities reported in October 2025.

Azad Kashmir produces more than 4,000 megawatts of electricity, while its daily requirement is estimated at only 350 to 400 megawatts. Despite this surplus, residents continue to face prolonged outages. Internet and mobile services remain unreliable even for users paying for 4G connections.

Protesters announced an indefinite sit-in in Rawalakot, stressing that all agreements made with the public must be honored in full and without delay. They called for an immediate end to collective punishment, the restoration of reliable electricity and communication services, and full respect for the basic rights and dignity of the people of Azad Kashmir.

The situation remains tense as residents await a response from the authorities.
