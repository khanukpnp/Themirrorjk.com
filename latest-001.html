Dangerous Escalation: US–Israel Strikes on Iran and Iran’s Retaliation Raise Fears of Wider War

By Sardar Nasir Aziz Khan | 1 March 2026 | Switzerland

Sardar Nasir Aziz Khan, Central Spokesperson of the United Kashmir People’s National Party, in a press statement said that the recent developments in the Middle East are deeply alarming and carry serious global consequences.

On 28 February 2026, military strikes by the United States and Israel against Iran sharply escalated tensions in the region. Tehran responded with missile and drone attacks on Israel and U.S. forces and bases in the Middle East. This cycle of action and retaliation has intensified fears of a broader confrontation.

He stated that when countries carry out airstrikes, launch missiles into each other’s territory, and draw in allies, the risk of wider conflict increases significantly. Such escalation can pull additional regional and global powers into direct confrontation, creating conditions that could potentially lead to a third world war.

Sardar Nasir Aziz Khan said war brings destruction on a massive scale. Hundreds of thousands of people could lose their lives. Millions may be forced to flee their homes. Global trade, energy markets, and essential services would face severe disruption, affecting not only the Middle East but the entire world.

He emphasized that civilians, particularly women, children, and the elderly, suffer first and suffer most in large‑scale conflicts. Rising food and fuel prices, destroyed infrastructure, and long‑term psychological and social trauma become the lasting legacy of war.

The world has repeatedly witnessed how localized violence can quickly spill across borders. In such circumstances, it is vital for world leaders to prioritize dialogue and negotiation instead of further military action. Peaceful solutions protect ordinary people and prevent cycles of revenge and chaos.

Sardar Nasir Aziz Khan urged all concerned countries to step back from confrontation and act with responsibility. “We must give peace a chance. Countries must choose diplomacy over war,” he added.
